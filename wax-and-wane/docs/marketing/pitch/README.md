## Only you can do these

1. **Your name or a studio name**, **your country**, and **one honest sentence on why you made this game**.
2. **A contact email** you check every day. A new address just for the game is a good idea.
3. **What you want from a publisher.** The kit says: marketing, testing, translation, consoles and phones, and
   no development money. Change it if that is not what you want.
4. **A price.** The press kit suggests $5.99 on Steam.
5. **Dates you can keep** for the demo and the Steam page.
6. **If you are under 18:** tell a parent or guardian now. They sign contracts and own the store and payment
   accounts. Publishers are used to this; hiding it is what causes trouble.
7. **One real run of the Windows build** on your ROG Ally. It has only been started on Linux here.

Put 1 to 5 into `answers.md`, `email.md` and the deck (every blank is in `[SQUARE BRACKETS]`; the deck has them
on the cover and slides 8, 9, 10, 11 and 12).

## Step by step

1. **Put the game online so a publisher can play in one click.**
   1. Make an account at itch.io (a parent's, if you are under 18).
   2. "Upload new project". Kind of project: **HTML**.
   3. Upload `WaxAndWane_2.8.0_web.zip`. Tick **"This file will be played in the browser"**.
   4. Viewport size: **1152 x 648**. Tick **Fullscreen button**.
   5. Also upload `WaxAndWane_2.8.0_windows.zip` as a download (tick Windows).
   6. itch asks whether the project uses generative AI: answer **yes** (see `made_with.md`).
   7. Visibility: **Restricted** while you test it, then **Public**. Copy the page link: that is your `[ITCH LINK]`.
2. **The video.** Nearly every form has a box for one. The trailer you have shows the old look from version 2.0.
   Tell me "update the trailer" and I will record it again from the new build. Upload it to YouTube as
   **Unlisted** and copy the link.
3. **Fill the blanks** (the list above).
4. **Share the deck.** Open the deck link, use its Share menu, and either share the link or download the PDF to
   attach.
5. **Check before the first send:** the itch.io page is live and plays, the Windows build has run once on a
   real PC, and every `[BLANK]` is filled.
6. **Send the first five** in `publishers.md`, one a day. Change one line in each email to say why you chose
   them.
7. **Write every send in the tracker** at the bottom of `publishers.md`. Follow up once after 10 working days.

## Honest odds, and what improves them

Most cold pitches get no answer, and this one has three things against it: it is a first game, nobody has
played it in public yet, and it was made with an AI assistant, which some publishers refuse.

What moves the odds most, in order:
1. **Real numbers.** Put the browser demo on itch.io and get it played. "200 people played it and the middle
   session lasted 14 minutes" is worth more than any slide.
2. **A Steam "Coming Soon" page** and its wishlist count ($100 fee, an adult's account).
3. **Writing to the right five** and saying why you chose each.

If no publisher bites, the game can still earn on its own: the store pages and texts for itch.io, Steam and
Google Play are already written in `docs/marketing/`.
