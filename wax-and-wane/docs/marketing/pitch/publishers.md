# Who to pitch (checked 10 November 2026)

Every row comes from the publisher's own pages. Six rows were opened and checked twice (marked **checked twice**);
the rest were read once by a research pass, so **open the page yourself before you send anything**: addresses and
forms change. "Not stated" means their page does not say.

How to use this list:
1. Start with the five **Send first** rows. Send one pitch a day, not all at once, so you can improve the next
   one from any reply.
2. Use the route they give (their form or their address). Never a general "info@" address.
3. Paste from `answers.md`. Attach or link the deck. Always give the browser link and the Windows build.
4. Write the date in the tracker at the bottom. Follow up once after 10 working days (`email.md`), then let it go.

## Do not pitch: they have said no to AI-made content

| Publisher | What they said | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Hooded Horse | The CEO says their contracts ban AI assets outright | Game Developer, 9 January 2026 (news report, not their own page) |
| Fireshine Games | The CEO is quoted saying they don't work with partners relying on generative AI or generative art | News reports only; the wording was not confirmed on the page |

Most publishers say nothing in public about AI. The ones who do speak are against it. So say plainly how this
game was made in the first email (`made_with.md` has the wording). A publisher who would refuse over it will
find out anyway, and finding out late costs you the deal and your name.

## What the stores ask about AI

- **Steam** (their Content Survey, read 10 November 2026): they ask about "the use of AI in creating content that
  ships with your game, and is consumed by players. This includes content such as artwork, sound, narrative,
  localization". "Efficiency gains through the use of these tools is not the focus of this section." For this
  game: the art and the in-game text must be described in that survey. Steam then shows a note on the store page.
- **itch.io**: has an AI disclosure question that tags the page. The current wording for games was not confirmed.
- **Google Play**: its AI policy is about apps that generate content for users, which this game does not do. No
  store-listing disclosure rule was confirmed.

## What their pages say they want

- **A build they can play once.** Raw Fury: "a reasonable expectation is that we'll only play the build the first
  time you send it". They also suggest "Skippable cutscenes/intro." and "Checkpoints you can load into in
  different parts of the build." (The opening and every scene skip with one press. There are no checkpoints: a
  night is about 9 minutes, and the Gentle level lets a reviewer see all four floors.)
- **What you want from them.** Armor Games, Versus Evil and Dear Villagers all ask. Have one clear sentence.
- **A gameplay video.** Nearly every form has a box for it.
- **A deck.** Raw Fury's six questions are a good test of any deck: what are you doing, why, who are you, where do
  you want to go, what do you need to get there, when will you be there.
- **Numbers, if you have them.** Graffiti asks for wishlists. You have none yet: say so, and say what you are
  doing about it (the free browser demo).
