# The cold scout review

Before any real publisher sees this kit, a reviewer with none of the reasoning behind it was told to act as a
publishing scout who gets fifty pitches a week and looks for the reason to say no. It was given only the deck,
the email, the answers, the "how it was made" statement, the screenshots and the fact sheet, plus what six
publishers say on their own pitch pages. It ran twice: once on the first kit, once on the kit after the fixes.

## First review: 5 out of 10

Its tracker line: "Cute candle-clock hook, but a first-time solo dev with no demo, no Steam page and no traction,
on a 0x72 plus AI-script art base. Pass; revisit with a live demo and wishlists."

| What it judged | Score | Its main point |
|---|---|---|
| The hook in the first ten seconds | 7 | Clear and quick |
| The art in the screenshots | 6 | Consistent and well lit, but three shots had an empty box that looked like a bug, every shot showed the same coins and bombs, and the featured guardian was a free-pack sprite |
| What the game is and how it plays | 7 | Clear, but "minutes" meant both clock minutes and real minutes |
| What makes it different | 5 | One idea, and it was told, not shown |
| Proof it is real and finished | 5 | No video or demo in the deck, links blank |
| The team | 2 | One anonymous first-time developer |
| The ask | 5 | Reasonable but vague |
| How the AI disclosure is handled | 6 | Honest, but apologetic in places |
| The commercial case | 4 | No wishlists, no price, no audience |
| The deck's craft | 6 | Clean; three places where text might not fit |

## What no rewrite can fix

These were its strongest objections, and they are about the world, not the wording:

1. **Nobody has played it yet.** No demo numbers, no wishlists, no Steam page.
2. **A first game by one developer**, with no track record.
3. **The art base**: a free tile set plus art made with an AI assistant. Some scouts will pass on that alone.
4. **No gameplay video in the deck.** The trailer shows the old version.
5. **The rooms look sparse and the sprites small** in screenshots. (The "bigger picture" work on the game is the
   fix for this; the screenshots get re-shot when it lands.)

The order to attack them: put the demo up and get it played (1), update the video (4), then pitch.

## Second review (after the fixes)

A second reviewer, again with none of the reasoning and none of the first review, scored the fixed kit
**4.5 out of 10**. Its tracker line: "Cute candle-clock roguelite, solo first-timer, AI-drawn art on 0x72 tiles,
no traction. Played the demo? Maybe. Pass unless it's fun in 3 minutes."

| What it judged | First | Second |
|---|---|---|
| The hook in the first ten seconds | 7 | 6 |
| The art in the screenshots | 6 | 5 |
| What the game is and how it plays | 7 | 8 |
| What makes it different | 5 | 6 |
| Proof it is real and finished | 5 | 4 |
| The team | 2 | 2 |
| The ask | 5 | 5 |
| How the AI disclosure is handled | 6 | 8 |
| The commercial case | 4 | 3 |
| The deck's craft | 6 | 7 |
| **Overall** | **5** | **4.5** |

Read it honestly: **the rewrite did not move the verdict.** The parts a rewrite can reach went up (clarity, what
makes it different, the disclosure, the deck's craft). The parts that decide a scout's answer did not, and two
different reviewers are within noise of each other on them: no players, no wishlists, no video, a first game, and
an art base some labels will not take. Both ended on the same word: pass, for now.

The second review's smaller points were fixed too: "finished" and "in polish" are now one phrase
("content-complete") in every file, the cover and email no longer claim the Steam Deck, the email names the
revenue-share ask and no longer pre-apologises, two near-empty screenshots were swapped for a fight and the shop,
and the fact sheet now carries every number the pitch leans on (hit costs, the trades, the diary and endings, the
text table, how it has been tested).

## What this means for you

The kit is ready to send the day these are true, and not before:

1. The browser demo is on itch.io and some strangers have played it (aim for 50 plays, and write down the
   middle session length).
2. The gameplay video shows the current version.
3. Every blank is filled.

Until then a pitch will most likely get no answer, and a publisher who has passed once rarely looks twice. The
demo costs nothing and is the single thing both reviewers asked for.
