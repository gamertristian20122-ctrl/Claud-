# Steam store page kit: Wax & Wane

Written for v2.0. Two kinds of markers:
- **[CHECK]** = depends on a v2.0 feature landing. Fix the number or delete the line before the page goes public.
- **[NEED]** = something only you know (your name, a real playtest number).

Steam rejects or hides pages that promise things the build doesn't have, so clear every marker first.

**Wax & Wane**

## 2. Short description (Steam limit: 300 characters)

**Use this one (283 characters):**

> A twin-stick roguelite where your health bar is a burning candle. Play Tallow, a little living candle in a haunted house, and shoot room to room from midnight to 2:00 AM. Every hit melts your wax. Every kill buys a little time back. Snuff the Hour Fiend before the clock strikes two.

Why: people skim this next to the capsule. The first sentence names the genre and the hook in one go, so a roguelite fan knows in four seconds whether to scroll.

**Backup (273 characters):**

> You are Tallow, a small living candle in a haunted house at midnight. Your wax is your health and your clock, and it burns from 12:00 to 2:00 AM. Monsters melt it faster and kills buy minutes back. Shoot room to room through four floors and snuff the Hour Fiend before two.

## 3. About This Game (paste into Steamworks > Store Page > Description)

Steam's editor takes this BBCode as is. Upload the GIFs first with "Insert image"; Steam gives you the `{STEAM_APP_IMAGE}` paths. Three short GIFs (under 3 MB each, 600 px wide) do more than any paragraph.

```
[img]{STEAM_APP_IMAGE}/extras/gif_candle_clock.gif[/img]

[h2]Your wax is your health and your clock[/h2]
Tallow is the last candle burning in the cellar of an old house, and at midnight the house wakes up. The candle in the corner of the screen counts from 12:00 AM to 2:00 AM, and it is the same wax that keeps you alive. Left alone, a full candle lasts about nine and a half minutes. Every hit melts more. Kills, cleared rooms and wax drippings buy minutes back.

Tallow also gets shorter as the wax goes, and the circle of light around you shrinks with it. A run that's going badly gets darker as well as harder.

[h2]Four floors before two o'clock[/h2]
Fight room to room down through the Cellar, the Crypt, the Chapel and the Belfry. Each floor is rebuilt every run from hand-made rooms. Doors stay shut until a room is clear, then the candelabras light and you pick a way on: a treasure room, a shop, the boss, or that cracked wall that's been seeping wax all floor. Bells toll at 1:00, 1:30 and 1:45, so you can hear how much night is left.
```

Notes on choices:
- Real item names (Twin Wick, Hand Bell, Old Rot) do the selling. Generic lines like "tons of items" could sit on any roguelite page.
- The mechanic gets the longest section because it's the one thing other roguelites don't have.
- No review quotes, awards or player counts until they exist.

## 6. Content survey (Steamworks > Content Survey)

**Mature content checkboxes:** tick none. Leave "Frequent Violence or Gore" unticked unless the v2.0 stains include red blood [CHECK].

**Describe the content (text box):**
> Cartoon fantasy violence. You play a small candle that shoots sparks at pixel-art monsters such as skeletons, zombies and demons in a haunted house. Defeated monsters vanish in smoke. The game has no blood, sexual content, strong language or real-money gambling.

**AI generated content disclosure (Pre-Generated section).** Steam's January 2026 update says behind-the-scenes coding assistants don't need disclosing, but anything players see or hear does. Much of this game's original art and music was made by scripts that an AI assistant wrote, so disclose it. Players who find undisclosed AI use after launch tend to say so in reviews, and a page that said it up front gives them nothing to catch.

> Wax & Wane is made by one developer working with an AI coding assistant. The assistant wrote most of the game's code, plus the scripts that draw much of the original pixel art (the hero, relic icons, effects and room art) and synthesize the music, all directed and reviewed by the developer. The dungeon tiles and creatures are hand-drawn CC0 art by 0x72 and the sound effects are CC0 packs by Kenney. Nothing is generated by AI while you play.

## 7. System requirements

Godot 4.7 GL Compatibility uses OpenGL 3.3 on desktop. The whole game draws at 384x216 and scales up, so it is light. [CHECK: run `--autotest --bench` on the slowest laptop you can borrow and adjust.]

**Windows: Minimum**
- OS: Windows 10 64-bit
- Processor: dual-core 2.0 GHz
- Memory: 2 GB RAM
- Graphics: OpenGL 3.3 support (Intel HD Graphics 4000 or newer)
- Storage: 300 MB available space
- Additional notes: 1280x720 display

**Windows: Recommended**
- OS: Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit
- Processor: quad-core 2.5 GHz
- Memory: 4 GB RAM
- Graphics: Intel UHD Graphics 620 or any dedicated GPU from 2014 on
- Storage: 300 MB available space
- Additional notes: gamepad recommended

**SteamOS + Linux**
- OS: SteamOS 3 or Ubuntu 22.04+, 64-bit
- Everything else as Windows, with Mesa or vendor drivers that support OpenGL 3.3

**macOS:** skip at launch. A Mac build needs Apple notarization ($99/year Apple Developer account) to open without scary warnings. Add it later if people ask.

Snapshots from store trackers (DekuDeals, SteamDB, store search), dates vary. Check the live prices the week you set yours.

### Recommendation: **$5.99 USD**

Reasons:
1. The $12 to $15 games have years of updates and tens of thousands of reviews. A debut from one person with zero reviews should sit well under them.
2. The $4.99 shelf is packed. $5.99 still reads as an impulse buy and leaves room for a 20% launch discount ($4.79) and a 50% sale ($2.99) later.
3. Rough take-home per full-price sale is about $3.50: $5.99, minus sales tax/VAT (built into prices in many countries), minus some refunds, minus Valve's 30%. At the launch discount, about $2.90. These are estimates; Steamworks shows the real numbers after launch.
4. Valve pays the $100 Steam Direct fee back once the game earns $1,000 on Steam.

Go to **$6.99** only if v2.0 ships with everything above (8 bosses, ~64 relics, 5 candles) and playtesters keep doing "one more run". Don't go above $7.99 for a first game.

**Regional prices:** accept Steam's recommended regional pricing. Hand-tuning countries isn't worth your time.

## 9. Launch discount plan

Steam's rules (Steamworks discount docs, checked Nov 2026):
- Launch discount: up to 40% off, running 7 to 14 days.
- No other discount in the first 30 days after release.
- 30 days between discounts, except Valve's seasonal sales.
- No discount for 30 days after you raise the base price.
- Custom discounts last 1 to 14 days.

The plan:
1. **Launch week:** 20% off ($4.79) for 7 days. Bigger launch cuts teach people to wait.
2. **Days 8 to 30:** full price. Post a small patch in this window; patch notes bring the page back into people's feeds.
3. **First seasonal sale after day 30:** 20 to 25% off.
4. **Next seasonal sales:** 30%, then 40%.
5. **Year-one floor:** 50% ($2.99), saved for the Winter Sale or the first big free update.
6. **Themed fests:** the haunted house fits Valve's October Scream Fest. Opt in through Steamworks > Upcoming Events [CHECK dates and eligibility].

## 10. Coming Soon page checklist (in order)

1. Pay the $100 Steam Direct fee. It can't be refunded, and Valve repays it at $1,000 earned.
2. Fill in identity, tax and bank details. Valve says this takes 15 to 30 days, so start now.
3. Create the app and fill in name, short description, About This Game, tags, genre, features, languages, content survey (with the AI disclosure) and system requirements.
4. Set the release date to a season or quarter ("Summer 2027") until you know the day.
5. Upload every capsule and library image in section 11, at least 5 screenshots, and the trailer.
6. Submit the store page for review. Valve takes 1 to 5 days.
7. Press publish to go "Coming Soon". The rule is at least two weeks before launch; aim for six months, since wishlists build slowly.
8. Add a "Wishlist on Steam" link to your itch.io page, Play listing, TikTok/YouTube bios and press kit.
9. Post a short Steam event ("The candle is lit: first look") so followers get something in their feed.
10. For Next Fest, make the demo as its own app with its own store page and review.
11. After launch, use Steam Curator Connect to send free keys to curators who cover roguelites.
