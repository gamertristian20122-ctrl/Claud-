## 2. Short description (Steam limit: 300 characters)

**Use this one (283 characters):**

> A twin-stick roguelite where your health bar is a burning candle. Play Tallow, a little living candle in a haunted house, and shoot room to room from midnight to 2:00 AM. Every hit melts your wax. Every kill buys a little time back. Snuff the Belfry's guardian before the clock strikes two.

Why: people skim this next to the capsule. The first sentence names the genre and the hook in one go, so a
roguelite fan knows in four seconds whether to scroll.

**Backup (266 characters):**

> You are Tallow, a little living candle in a haunted house. Your wax is your health and your clock: it burns from 12:00 to 2:00 AM, monsters melt it faster and kills buy minutes back. Burn through four floors, beat the clock to 1:00 AM and a secret fifth floor opens.

## 3. About This Game (paste into Steamworks > Store Page > Description)

Steam's editor takes this BBCode as is. Upload `loop_fight.gif` first with "Insert image" (made by
`./tools/make_trailer.sh all`, 1.1 MB; Steam gives you the `{STEAM_APP_IMAGE}` path).

```
[img]{STEAM_APP_IMAGE}/extras/loop_fight.gif[/img]

[h2]Your wax is your health and your clock[/h2]
Tallow is the last candle burning in the cellar of an old house, and at midnight the house wakes up. The candle in the corner of the screen counts from 12:00 AM to 2:00 AM, and it is the same wax that keeps you alive. Left alone, a full candle lasts eight minutes. Every hit melts more. Kills, cleared rooms and wax drippings buy minutes back.

Tallow also gets shorter as the wax goes, and the circle of light around you shrinks with it. A run that's going badly gets darker as well as harder.

[h2]Four floors before two o'clock, and one secret[/h2]
Fight room to room down through the Cellar, the Crypt, the Chapel and the Belfry. Each floor is rebuilt every run from 69 hand-made rooms. Doors stay shut until a room is clear, then the candelabras light and you pick a way on: a treasure room, the Tallow Merchant's shop, the guardian, or that cracked wall that's been seeping wax all floor. Bells toll at 1:00, 1:30 and 1:45, so you can hear how much night is left.

Snuff the Belfry's guardian by 1:00 AM and a ladder of light opens to a secret fifth floor, the Clock Tower, where the Keeper of Hours waits with the true ending.

[h2]Builds that get out of hand[/h2]
[list]
[*][b]64 relics.[/b] Twin Wick gives you a second flame, Will-o'-Wisp makes your shots seek, Hot Drip leaves burning wax wherever you walk. Pick up Sharp Wick and Ember Heart on top of Twin Wick and you're firing two piercing flames that set everything they pass through alight.
[*][b]Six Candle Rites.[/b] Hold three relics from the same Rite (Hearth, Clock, Moth, Many Wicks, Household or Seance) for a set bonus you can see on Tallow. Finish the Rite of the Hearth and burning foes burst into embers.
[*][b]Eight keepsakes.[/b] Carry one charged item, filled by clearing rooms. The Hand Bell freezes the house for three seconds. The Winding Key turns the clock back ten minutes.
[*][b]The Smoke Altar.[/b] After some guardians you can buy a strong relic with minutes cut off the top of your candle for the rest of the night. If the run is going badly, the Vigil offers a free gift instead.
[/list]

[h2]The house wants you snuffed[/h2]
[list]
[*][b]35 kinds of lodger[/b]: hoppers that splash water on your flame, archers, chargers, ring-casters, summoners, flask-throwers, a waxwork bride who only moves when no one looks, and a treasure chest that bites.
[*][b]Marked foes[/b] glow ember, gilded, sooty or chilled. They take more killing and pay out wax, coins or bombs.
[*][b]13 guardians and 6 house champions.[/b] Three guardians wait on each floor and the run's seed picks one, each announced with its own versus card: Old Rot, the Wax Mother or the Coal Furnace in the Cellar, up to the Hour Fiend's sweeping clock hands, the Great Pendulum or the Weathercock's gales in the Belfry.
[*][b]Omens[/b] twist a floor and always pay you back. Cold Draught melts you faster, but drippings are richer. Smoky Halls shortens your light, but every room gives twice.
[/list]

[h2]Rooms with a price[/h2]
Five special rooms can turn up on a floor. Ring the Seance bell and the lodgers answer in waves. The Cracked Mirror offers a strong relic for fifteen minutes of wax, for good. The Music Box takes three coins a crank and plays what it likes. The Study lays out three gifts (a keepsake, tonics or the floor's map); take one and the rest crumble to dust. The Wax Font drinks minutes and always pays on the sixth offering.

[h2]Reasons to light another candle[/h2]
[list]
[*][b]The Candle Box.[/b] Unlock five more candles, each with its own clock. The Tea Light is lit at 12:40 and fires fast. The Party Candle has two flames and thin wax. The Altar Pillar is lit at 11:45 and hits hard. The Black Taper is hard mode. The Moonwick is the Clock Tower's secret.
[*][b]Tonight's Candle.[/b] One seeded house a night: the same house for every player, scored on your first try. Or type in a friend's seed.
[*][b]Hollows.[/b] Every floor hides a secret room behind a cracked wall. Bring a bomb.
[*][b]The Journal.[/b] Every relic, lodger, guardian and room you have met, your records and your last 20 runs. Unseen pages stay as silhouettes.
[*][b]52 achievements[/b], tracked in the game. Eighteen relics and two keepsakes move into the house as you earn them.
[/list]

[h2]Keyboard, mouse or gamepad[/h2]
Play with keyboard and mouse or a gamepad; the button hints follow whatever you touched last. The picture is drawn at 384 x 216 and scaled up in whole steps, so it stays sharp on a 1080p monitor and on a Steam Deck. Quit in the middle of a run and the game saves; pick it up from the title screen. A run takes about 5 to 20 minutes.

[h2]Who made this[/h2]
Wax & Wane is made by one developer working with Claude, an AI assistant. The dungeon tiles and about 20 of the lodgers come from 0x72's free DungeonTileset II (CC0). The music is the Ninja Adventure pack by Pixel-boy and AAA (CC0), and the sound effects are layered from Kenney's free packs. Tallow, the house's own lodgers and guardians, the relics, the rooms and the effects were made for this game. The AI disclosure on this page says exactly how AI was used.

Add Wax & Wane to your wishlist and Steam will email you the day it comes out.
```

## 5. Genre and feature checkboxes

**Genre:** Action, Indie. Leave RPG, Casual, Adventure and the rest unticked.

**Features to tick at launch:**
- **Single-player.**
- **Controller support:** tick **Full Controller Support** once you have played a whole run and every menu
  (title, Candle Box, Journal, Settings, pause, results) on the ROG Ally's own controls without touching the
  screen. If any menu needs the touchscreen or a mouse, tick **Partial Controller Support** instead.
- **Steam Cloud:** tick it only after Auto-Cloud is set up (no code needed). Steamworks > App Admin >
  Cloud > Steam Cloud Settings: byte quota 1048576, number of files 10. Then Root Overrides / Auto-Cloud
  paths:

  | Root | Subdirectory | Pattern | OS |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | `WinAppDataRoaming` | `WaxAndWane` | `*.cfg` | Windows |
  | `LinuxXdgDataHome` | `WaxAndWane` | `*.cfg` | Linux (and Steam Deck) |

  The 2.0.0 build saves `save.cfg` and `run.cfg` in exactly those folders (`project.godot`:
  `custom_user_dir_name = WaxAndWane`). Test once: play on one PC, then check the save appears on a second one.
- **Family Sharing:** on by default; leave it on.

**Do not tick at launch:**
- **Steam Achievements.** The build has no Steamworks connection yet (the GodotSteam plugin is not in it), so
  the 52 achievements are shown in the game only. Tick it after an update adds GodotSteam, the 52 achievements
  are created in Steamworks with the same API names, and each has its 64x64 earned and unearned icons.
  In the page text, achievements are described as in-game.
- Steam Leaderboards, Trading Cards, Remote Play: later, if you add them.

**Languages:** English, interface + subtitles. Only the trailer has a voice, so leave "Full audio" empty.

**Steam Deck:** after launch, Valve tests the game for Deck Verified. The game already shows gamepad glyphs and
scales to 1280 x 800.

## 6. Content survey (Steamworks > Content Survey)

**Mature content checkboxes:** tick none. The violence is cartoonish and small (see the text below); it does
not need "Frequent Violence or Gore".

**Describe the content (text box):**
> Cartoon fantasy violence. You play a small candle that shoots sparks at pixel-art monsters (skeletons, zombies, imps, ghosts, living furniture) in a haunted house. Defeated monsters burst into a few tiny pixel chunks and leave flat coloured splats on the floor, some of them red. Nothing is realistic or detailed. There is no sexual content, no strong language and no real-money gambling; a music box in the house trades in-game coins for a random small reward.

**AI generated content disclosure (Pre-Generated section).** Steam's January 2026 update says behind-the-scenes
coding tools don't need disclosing, but anything players see or hear does. Most of this game's original art
was drawn by scripts an AI assistant wrote, and the trailer has an AI voice, so disclose it. Players who find
undisclosed AI use after launch tend to say so in reviews; a page that said it up front gives them nothing
to catch. Paste this:

> Wax & Wane is made by one developer working with Claude, an AI assistant. The assistant wrote the game's code and the in-game text with the developer, and it wrote the small programs that draw the original pixel art (Tallow, the house's own creatures and guardians, the relics, rooms and effects). The developer checked every picture and kept, changed or threw out each one. The sound effects were layered and processed from free CC0 sound packs by the same kind of program, with a few synthesized ones, and the two short jingles were synthesized too. The music is not AI: it is the CC0 Ninja Adventure soundtrack by Pixel-boy and AAA, and the dungeon tiles and about 20 creatures are hand-drawn CC0 art by 0x72. The narration in the trailer is an AI-generated voice (Kokoro TTS, an open text-to-speech model). Nothing is generated by AI while you play.

**Live-Generated section:** No.

## 7. System requirements

The game draws at 384 x 216 and scales up, with Godot 4.7's Compatibility renderer: OpenGL 3.3, and on
Windows it falls back to Direct3D 11 (ANGLE, built into the game) when the OpenGL driver is missing or broken.
These are safe estimates for a game this light. Before you publish them, run the Windows zip on the ROG Ally
(POST_IT.md, Steam step 3); if it runs well there, publish them as they are.

**Windows: Minimum**
- OS: Windows 10 64-bit
- Processor: dual-core 2.0 GHz
- Memory: 2 GB RAM
- Graphics: OpenGL 3.3 or Direct3D 11 (Intel HD Graphics 4000 or newer)
- Storage: 300 MB available space
- Additional notes: 1280x720 display

**Windows: Recommended**
- OS: Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit
- Processor: quad-core 2.5 GHz
- Memory: 4 GB RAM
- Graphics: Intel UHD Graphics 620 or any dedicated GPU from 2014 on
- Storage: 300 MB available space
- Additional notes: gamepad recommended

**SteamOS + Linux**
- OS: SteamOS 3 or Ubuntu 22.04+, 64-bit
- Everything else as Windows, with Mesa or vendor drivers that support OpenGL 3.3

**macOS:** skip at launch. A Mac build needs Apple notarization ($99/year Apple Developer account) to open
without scary warnings. Add it later if people ask.

### Recommendation: **$5.99 USD**

Reasons:
1. The $12 to $15 games have years of updates and tens of thousands of reviews. A debut from one person with zero reviews should sit well under them.
2. The $4.99 shelf is packed. $5.99 still reads as an impulse buy and leaves room for a 20% launch discount ($4.79) and a 50% sale ($2.99) later.
3. Rough take-home per full-price sale is about $3.50: $5.99, minus sales tax/VAT (built into prices in many countries), minus some refunds, minus Valve's 30%. At the launch discount, about $2.90. These are estimates; Steamworks shows the real numbers after launch.
4. Valve pays the $100 Steam Direct fee back once the game earns $1,000 on Steam.

The game has 13 guardians, 64 relics, 6 candles, 5 special rooms and a secret floor, so **$6.99** is also
fair if playtesters keep doing "one more run". Don't go above $7.99 for a first game.

**Regional prices:** accept Steam's recommended regional pricing. Hand-tuning countries isn't worth your time.

## 9. Launch discount plan

Steam's rules (Steamworks discount docs, checked Nov 2026):
- Launch discount: up to 40% off, running 7 to 14 days.
- No other discount in the first 30 days after release.
- 30 days between discounts, except Valve's seasonal sales.
- No discount for 30 days after you raise the base price.
- Custom discounts last 1 to 14 days.

The plan:
1. **Launch week:** 20% off ($4.79) for 7 days. Bigger launch cuts teach people to wait.
2. **Days 8 to 30:** full price. Post a small patch in this window; patch notes bring the page back into people's feeds.
3. **First seasonal sale after day 30:** 20 to 25% off.
4. **Next seasonal sales:** 30%, then 40%.
5. **Year-one floor:** 50% ($2.99), saved for the Winter Sale or the first big free update.
6. **Themed fests:** the haunted house fits Valve's October Scream Fest. Opt in through Steamworks > Upcoming Events when Valve opens sign-ups (they post the dates there).

## 10. Coming Soon page checklist (in order)

1. Pay the $100 Steam Direct fee. It can't be refunded, and Valve repays it at $1,000 earned.
2. Fill in identity, tax and bank details. Valve says this takes 15 to 30 days, so start now.
3. Create the app and fill in name, short description, About This Game, tags, genre, features, languages, content survey (with the AI disclosure) and system requirements.
4. Set the release date to a season or quarter ("Summer 2027") until you know the day.
5. Upload every capsule and library image in section 11, at least 5 screenshots, and the Steam trailer (`build/videos/steam/trailer_60s_steam.mp4`, the one that ends on WISHLIST ON STEAM).
6. Submit the store page for review. Valve takes 1 to 5 days.
7. Press publish to go "Coming Soon". The rule is at least two weeks before launch; aim for six months, since wishlists build slowly.
8. Add a "Wishlist on Steam" link to your itch.io page, Play listing, TikTok/YouTube bios and press kit (`POST_IT.md` has the lines).
9. Post a short Steam event ("The candle is lit: first look") so followers get something in their feed.
10. Later, optional: a Next Fest demo has to be built first (2.0.0 has no demo mode) and goes up as its own app with its own review.
11. After launch, use Steam Curator Connect to send free keys to curators who cover roguelites.
