# Wax & Wane: the story of the house (v2.5 story bible)

The owner asked for "a real story that is in the game". This is the bible every page, ending and future line
is checked against. It is built out of what the game already says (every lodger's and guardian's lore, the
candles, the intro, the endings, the hollows, the chalk): nothing below contradicts an existing line, so no
old line was changed.

## In one breath

An old house runs on a great clock. Every night at twelve a brass Keeper winds it, and the house gets up and
does its rounds: the pot simmers, the organ plays, the Gaoler locks the crypt, the bell rings at one. In this
house, anything done every night for long enough starts to do itself, so the rounds have gone on for three
hundred years, long after the people who did them were gone. **Martha Fenn**, the last housekeeper, wanted
the house to be allowed to rest. She was too old to climb to the clock, so she made a candle, because a
candle is the one thing that knows how to end. She lit him every night for a year until he could light
himself. His name is **Tallow**.

## The rule of the house

> In this house, anything done every night for long enough starts to do itself.

Everything follows from this one rule:
- The **lodgers** (the house's own word for whatever lives in it) are chores, habits and things that were
  kept too long: the organ plays itself, the Stockpot stirs itself, the Cog Sentries keep a watch nobody
  asked for any more. "Nothing is left inside its robes but the habit of keeping watch" (the Hollow Warden)
  is the whole house in one line.
- **Why the house wakes at midnight:** "Wind the clock at twelve" is the first line in the Book of Rounds,
  written by the clockmaker. The Keeper of Hours has done it every night for three hundred years. When the
  clock is wound, the rounds start, and everything kept in the house gets up to do its chore.
- **Why two o'clock:** at two the rounds end. Anything still burning goes out, and the house puts everything
  back. Not always where it was: that is why the rooms are never in the same order two nights running.
- **Snuffing a lodger** only puts it to bed until twelve. Nobody really gets hurt in this house; they get
  tired. That keeps the game's fights warm instead of grim.
- **Wax is time** because the clock runs on borrowed minutes: "every swing takes a minute from someone"
  (the Great Pendulum). Candles here burn fast, and the people who lived here grew old quickly.

## Tallow

- Made by Martha from the clean top of the Wax Mother's pool, taken while she slept, and an old wick from
  the chapel. Stubby, lopsided, cried wax when the match was struck ("Cries wax when he is scared and keeps
  going anyway"). The Wax Mother wants him back ("She wants you back"): he is one of her drips.
- **Who lights him:** Martha, every night at twelve for a year ("good evening", then blown out again), until
  the house learned the habit. Now the match on the title screen strikes itself at twelve and Tallow wakes in
  the cellar: "the last candle in the cellar". The rule that traps the house is the rule that gives him a new
  try every night.
- **Why a candle:** everything in the house has forgotten how to stop. A candle never forgets: it burns, it
  gives its light, and then it goes out. Tallow is sent up to show the house that ending is allowed.
- **The other candles** (Tea Light, Party Candle, Altar Pillar, Black Taper, Moonwick) are candles of the house
  that wake once Tallow has shown them the way: the tea lights the Book of Rounds lights in the crypt at twenty
  to one, a party candle from a birthday nobody came to, the chapel's altar pillar, a taper from the vestry,
  and Moonwick, lit by the moon through the tower's slats. The diary is about Tallow; any candle can read it.

## The Keeper of Hours

The clockmaker's brass servant, with a dial for a face. One order, three hundred years old: never let the
clock run down. It has wound the house clock every night since ("It winds the house, every night"). It is not
wicked: it is the most faithful thing in the house, and the most tired. It keeps its ladder pulled up and
lets it down only for someone **early** (the Belfry beaten by half the night: 1:00 AM on a four-floor night).
"Tonight it wants to wind you": it sees a candle that measures time and wants to keep him going forever, like
everything else. Stilling it is telling it, at last, that it can stop.

## The Hour Fiend and the Pendulum

A clock that keeps every minute has to get them from somewhere. Each swing of the Great Pendulum (the clock's
heart) takes a minute from someone in the house. In the gaps between its ticks lives what Cook called the
Hour Fiend: the clockmaker's fear of wasted time, grown teeth. It eats the minutes you waste and wants the
clock to strike two, because at two anything still burning goes out. "Some say the Pendulum has a master."

| **The Chapel** | Services every evening, by candlelight. | THE HOLLOW WARDEN, THE MELTED CHOIR (sang by candlelight so long their faces ran), THE ORGANIST (Mr. Pennock's organ). |

## Martha's diary: 24 pages

Torn out and scattered by the house. Pages turn up roughly in story order (the next few missing pages are
the candidates), so the diary reads like a life even when it is found piecemeal. The full text is in
`scripts/game/diary.gd`; where each page turns up is in `docs/streams/v25_story.md`.

1. **My first night**: Martha at fourteen; the house gets up at twelve; Cook hands her a broom.
2. **The Book of Rounds**: every chore written down; the first line in the clockmaker's hand.
3. **The house's one rule**: anything done every night for long enough starts to do itself.
4. **Under the cellar stairs**: Old Rot, the Wax Mother. *(the Cellar)*
5. **Cook's one law**: the stockpot never goes off the boil. *(the Kitchen)*
6. **Down in the crypt**: the Gaoler, the Sexton, the Ossuary. *(the Crypt)*
7. **The merchant**: the Tallow Merchant, whose name is extra.
8. **Mr. Pennock**: the organ plays his funeral by itself. *(the Chapel)*
9. **Silence**: the Head Librarian shushes Cook two floors down. *(the Library)*
10. **Miss Tibbet's dress**: the wedding that never happened. *(the Attic)*
11. **The great bell**: Big Walter, the weathercock, something huge swinging. *(the Belfry)*
12. **Below stairs**: nine of them once; the house kept doing their chores.
13. **The family**: the seaside, letters, money, nothing.
14. **The seance**: the maids ask the house what it wants; every lodger answers at once.
15. **Wilf**: the boot boy in the coal bin; snuffing only puts a lodger to bed.
16. **The clockmaker**: afraid of a wasted minute.
17. **Borrowed time**: every swing takes a minute from someone.
18. **Between the ticks**: the Hour Fiend, and why two o'clock.
19. **The Keeper of Hours**: it was never told it could stop; the ladder comes down for someone early. *(the Clock Tower)*
20. **The hammer**: Martha at nineteen; the house puts everything back, not always where it was.
21. **What a candle knows**: it burns, gives its light and goes out. "I am going to make a candle."
22. **Good tallow**: Tallow is made; the Wax Mother will want him back.
23. **Every night at twelve**: a year of lighting him; the chalk; the hollows sealed with wax.
24. **For Tallow**: her letter: go up past the bells and tell them all they can stop. *(the Keeper's first fall)*

## Voice

- Short sentences, plain words, one specific funny detail per page ("The stack has opinions now"; "He said
  that was extra"). Warm, a little sad, never gothic purple, never generic ("darkness", "evil", "ancient
  power" are banned).
- Martha writes like a busy housekeeper: practical, dry, fond of everyone, especially the ones she grumbles
  about. She never explains magic; she reports what the house does, like weather.
- Lodgers and guardians are never villains. They are tired, stubborn, faithful things that forgot how to stop.
- Every new player-facing string goes through `L.t(...)` (ROADMAP_V2 4a) so the v2.6 translations get them.
